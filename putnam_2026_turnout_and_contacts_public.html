01 Did the turnout score predict who voted?

A good sorter, a poor forecaster

VAN assigns every registered voter a turnout score from 0 to 100, fixed early in the cycle, meant to read as their probability of voting. This checks it against what 23,570 people on the Putnam County file actually did on 3 November 2026, when 34.1% of them voted.

Those are two separate questions. Does a higher score mean a likelier voter? And when the score says 70, do 70% of them turn out? The answer here is yes to the first and no to the second.

The ranking works

Pick a voter and a non-voter at random and the voter holds the higher score 86.8% of the time, counting a tied pair as half — an AUC of 0.868 (95% CI 0.863–0.873). Turnout climbs almost perfectly in step with the score: a linear trend accounts for 96.9% of the association between the two (Cochran–Armitage χ² = 8,921 on 1 df). For sorting a universe into who is worth a knock, the score does its job.

The number is too high, everywhere

Taken at face value the score expected 10,735 voters out of this universe. 8,037 turned out — it over-predicted by 34%. This is not noise in one or two bands: every band came in under its own score, and 18 of 20 do so beyond a 5% false-discovery rate. The gap is widest in the middle of the scale, where the 70–75 band scored 72 and turned out 51.6%.

Fitting the observed rates against the score on a log-odds scale gives a calibration slope of 0.83 (perfect is 1.00) and an intercept of -0.81 (perfect is 0.00) — a scale that is both shifted down and flattened. A two-parameter correction of that form tracks the real rates within a few points across most of the range, so the information is there; it is the labeling that is off.

03 What came of it

Dialing is not reaching

The contact log records that an attempt was made. The dialer's own log records what came of it — and most of those dials never had a volunteer on them at all.

Two kinds of dial. Of 19,884 dials in the general, only 3,370 had a volunteer on the line. The other 16,514 were the dialer working on its own, and they are a different thing entirely: not one of them was ever logged as a conversation, a refusal or a wrong number, while “Dropped” and “Error” appear nowhere else. Mean duration is 47 seconds on a dial that reached someone against about two seconds on an automated one.

Mixing the two gives a pick-up rate of 6.2%. Counted over the dials a volunteer actually handled, it is 36.5%. The second number is the one that describes the work.

04 Who we reached, and who voted

Setting outreach against the vote record

Every day from the first day of early voting to election day, a list of people who had already voted was loaded against the contact file. Set that against the people the program reached and you get a first read on how much of the outreach landed on people who turned out.

These counts are a floor, not a rate. The vote record on file is incomplete — 7,836 credits loaded between 6 October and 3 November, and more are still to be added. Final vote history is forthcoming. Read “no credit on file” as not yet matched to a vote, not as did not vote: some of those people did vote and are not yet recorded.

This is not a measure of what contact achieved. The program chose whom to call, text and visit using the turnout score, so the people it reached were always going to vote more than the county as a whole, and the channels differ because the people assigned to them differ. Separating the effect of being contacted from the effect of being the sort of person who gets contacted needs vote history for the people who were not contacted too, matched on turnout score. That data is not in this report.

05 Methods and limits

Where the numbers come from

Turnout scores and vote history are a VAN export for the Putnam County universe, cross-tabulated at 5-point score thresholds. The export is cumulative — each row counts everyone at that score or higher — so the bands here are built by differencing adjacent rows. Contact records come from two places: the program's VAN contact history, which records that an attempt happened, and the dialer's and texter's own logs, which record what came of it.

Event rows, and why they are not attempts

7,836 records of type “Event” are vote credits rather than outreach: one per person, bulk-loaded daily from 6 October through 3 November, 3,965 of them on 3 November alone. They are excluded from every attempt and channel figure, because nobody was contacted by them, and used instead as the record of who voted. Excluding them is why the universe reached is 12,519 rather than 15,454; the 2,935 people who appear only as a credit were never contacted. The record is incomplete and final vote history is still to come.

What counts as reaching someone

A dial reached a person when the caller logged an outcome only a person can produce — a conversation, a refusal, a wrong number, not home, moved, deceased, or no language in common. The dialer also carries its own answering-machine detection, which flagged 1,299 human answers against the caller's 1,231. The two agree on 761 dials and disagree on 1,008, so the figures here are the campaign's own record rather than the machine's.

What is aggregated, and why

The contact figures on this page are counts only. Individual voter identifiers are not published here, nor carried in this file's data, and volunteers appear by rank rather than by name. There is nothing on this page that resolves to a person.

Limits worth stating

Placing each band at its midpoint assumes scores are spread evenly inside the band; pinning them to the floor or the ceiling instead moves the headline ratio only from 0.78 to 0.72, against 0.75 as reported, so that assumption is not driving the result. This is one election in one county: a score built to predict a presidential year will look miscalibrated against a midterm even when it is working as designed, and nothing here separates those two explanations. The contact data describes a universe that was itself being worked, so these are not an untouched control group.

The two contact sources do not reconcile exactly. 12% of dials in the general (2,370 of 19,884) carry a voter ID that appears nowhere in the VAN contact history, so the dial happened but no contact record was written for it. Those dials are counted here rather than discarded, which is why the dial total runs ahead of the phone attempts in the contact log.
